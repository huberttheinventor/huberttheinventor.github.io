CEILING / THE DOUBLING TIME CARD

The doubling time card

The rule in one line: anything that grows at a steady rate r % and compounds all the time doubles in about 69.3 ÷ r units of time.

doubling time  T = ln 2 / (r/100) = 69.3 / r        (continuous compounding)
paid once per period:  T = ln 2 / ln(1 + r/100)     (a little longer; the "rule of 72" approximates this one)

Where 69.3 comes from: ln 2 = 0.6931..., the natural logarithm of 2, which is the logarithm with base e. It is e's logarithm because continuous growth at rate r multiplies an amount by e^(r·t) after time t, and e^(r·T) = 2 gives T = ln 2 / r.

Use 69.3 when growth is continuous (populations, bacteria, a price index quoted as a continuous rate, a model's exp()). Use 72 for money paid once a year: above about 4 % it is closer to the exact column than 69.3, it is almost exact near 8 %, and 72 divides evenly by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 and 12.

Where e comes from (the film): €1 at 100 % for a year, the interest paid in n equal slices, ends at (1 + 1/n)^n.

e = 2.718281828459045... The total rises with every finer split and never reaches e. At any rate r the same ceiling is e^r: 7 % compounded ever more finely ends at e^0.07 = 1.0725 per year, not 1.07.

Try it yourself (Python 3, no libraries):

from math import e, log

for n in (1, 2, 12, 365, 31_536_000):
    print(f"{n:>10} payments a year: {(1 + 1/n)**n:.6f}   (e = {e:.6f})")

for r in (2, 5, 7, 10):
    print(f"{r:>2}%: doubles in {log(2) / (r/100):.1f} (continuous), "
          f"{log(2) / log(1 + r/100):.1f} (yearly), rule of 69.3 says {69.3 / r:.1f}")
