The determinant is a volume.

Everyone memorises ad − bc and nobody says what it measures. Pull a matrix's columns out as vectors, look at the solid they span, and the determinant is that solid's volume. Every other fact about it falls out of that one picture.

The whole mental model, one picture. Everything after it is annotation.

Take it with you: the map as an image · as a vector

Nº001

The columns are vectors. The vectors span a solid.

Take a 3×3 matrix and read it column by column. Each column is three numbers, which is a point in space, which is a vector from the origin. Draw all three and you have three arrows leaving one corner. The shape they span — the squashed box with those three arrows as its edges — is called a parallelepiped, and its volume is the absolute value of the determinant.

The dimension is not special. Two columns span a parallelogram and you get an area. Three span a solid and you get a volume. n columns span an n-dimensional analogue and you get an n-volume. Same measurement, same number, more axes.

Nº002

Zero volume means a dimension went missing.

The determinant is zero exactly when that solid has flattened. This is where the picture starts paying for itself, because flatness is not just a smaller volume — it is a statement about the columns themselves.

Here is the argument. Take n−1 of your column vectors and call the shape they span the base. The remaining vector's height above that base is the solid's height, and volume is base times height. If the volume is zero then the height is zero, which means that last vector lies inside the base — it is a combination of the others. It was never adding a new direction. The column span has one fewer dimension than it appears to.

And if you object that the volume could be zero because the base itself collapsed, the same argument applies to the base, one dimension down, until you reach two vectors in a plane where a zero-length base means you lost a vector outright.

Nº003

Which is why a zero determinant has no inverse.

An inverse undoes a transformation: hand it any output and it hands back the one input that produced it. That is only possible if no two inputs ever land on the same output.

A flattened image is lower dimensional than the space it came from, so the map has to be squashing distinct inputs onto the same point — there is not enough room on the other side for them to stay separate. Several inputs, one output, nothing to hand back. The matrix is not invertible, and the determinant told you so with a single number.

That is the whole reason the determinant is worth carrying around. It is not a formula to be memorised, it is a volume scaling factor: the amount by which a transformation stretches or squashes space. Zero means it flattened space, and flattened space cannot be unflattened.
