TRENDLINE / THE SPRING CARD

The spring card

Least squares in one picture: tie every dot to a straight ruler with an ideal spring that pulls only straight up or down, let go, and the ruler comes to rest on the best-fit line.

spring pull     = k × gap          (twice the gap, twice the pull)
spring energy   = ½ k × gap²       (twice the gap, four times the energy)
total energy    = ½ k × (sum of squared gaps)
least squares   = the line that makes the sum of squared gaps as small as it can be
mean squared error (MSE) = (sum of squared gaps) ÷ (number of dots)

k is the spring's stiffness: how hard it pulls per unit of stretch. Every spring here has the same k.

So the ruler at rest, the least-squares line and the lowest-MSE line are the same line.

Why the ruler stops where it does

At rest, the pulls cancel in two ways at once:

  1. The upward pulls equal the downward pulls: the gaps above the line add up to the gaps below it (sum of gaps = 0).
  2. The pulls do not turn the ruler either: sum of (x × gap) = 0.

Those two balance conditions are exactly the equations that give the least-squares slope and intercept:

slope     b = Σ (x − x̄)(y − ȳ) ÷ Σ (x − x̄)²
intercept a = ȳ − b x̄

One dot 3 gaps away pulls as hard as three dots 1 gap away, and its spring holds 9 times the energy of each of them.

Worked example (eight illustrative dots, invented numbers: pizza size in inches 8, 10, 10, 12, 12, 14, 16, 18 against price 6.0, 7.5, 8.5, 9.0, 10.5, 11.0, 13.0, 14.5): the best-fit line is price = 0.8333 × size − 0.4167. Drag the fourth dot (12 inches) up by 8 and the new best-fit line is price = 0.7821 × size + 1.2244; at 12 inches the line rises from 9.58 to 10.61, and that one spring now holds 84.6% of the total energy. Output of springs_check.py below.

What to do with it

  • When a fitted line looks wrong, look at the dot with the biggest gap (the longest spring) first. One bad point can drag a squared-error fit.
  • If your data has real outliers you cannot remove, losses that grow more slowly than the square exist: Huber loss is "less sensitive to outliers in data than the squared error loss" (Wikipedia, Huber loss), and scikit-learn's HuberRegressor "gives a lesser weight to them".

Where the picture is exact, and where it is not

The picture is exact only for ideal springs: zero length when relaxed (so the pull is exactly k × gap), each one kept vertical (so it measures the up-and-down gap, which is what least squares uses), all with the same stiffness, a weightless ruler, and a little friction so it settles instead of wobbling forever. Real springs have a resting length and swing sideways, and then the rest position is not exactly the least-squares line. Springs that can slide freely and pull at any angle measure the shortest distance instead, which gives a different line.

Check it yourself (plain Python, no libraries)

springs_check.py (below) simulates the damped ruler with ideal vertical springs and compares it with the least-squares formula.

# plain Python: ideal vertical zero-rest-length springs vs least squares
x = [8,10,10,12,12,14,16,18]; y = [6.0,7.5,8.5,9.0,10.5,11.0,13.0,14.5]
n=len(x)
def ls(x,y):
    mx=sum(x)/n; my=sum(y)/n
    b=sum((xi-mx)*(yi-my) for xi,yi in zip(x,y))/sum((xi-mx)**2 for xi in x); return my-b*mx,b
def springs(x,y,k=1.0,steps=1500000,dt=0.004,damp=1.0):
    a=b=va=vb=0.0  # ruler starts flat and crooked, weightless, rigid
    I=sum(xi*xi for xi in x)/n
    for _ in range(steps):
        r=[yi-(a+b*xi) for xi,yi in zip(x,y)]
        Fa=k*sum(r); Tb=k*sum(ri*xi for ri,xi in zip(r,x))
        va+=dt*(Fa/n-damp*va); vb+=dt*(Tb/(n*I)-damp*vb); a+=dt*va; b+=dt*vb
    return a,b
E=lambda a,b,yy=y: 0.5*sum((yi-(a+b*xi))**2 for xi,yi in zip(x,yy))
a0,b0=ls(x,y); a1,b1=springs(x,y)
print("least squares a=%.6f b=%.6f"%(a0,b0)); print("springs at rest a=%.6f b=%.6f"%(a1,b1))
r=[yi-(a0+b0*xi) for xi,yi in zip(x,y)]
print("sum of gaps %.2e  sum x*gap %.2e"%(sum(r),sum(ri*xi for ri,xi in zip(r,x))))
print("energy at LS %.5f; nudged up %.5f; tilted %.5f"%(E(a0,b0),E(a0+0.2,b0),E(a0,b0+0.02)))
y2=list(y); y2[3]+=8; a2,b2=ls(x,y2)
print("dot 4 dragged up by 8: a=%.4f b=%.4f; ruler height at x=12: %.3f -> %.3f"%(a2,b2,a0+b0*12,a2+b2*12))
r2=[yi-(a2+b2*xi) for xi,yi in zip(x,y2)]; print("gaps after drag", [round(v,3) for v in r2])
print("outlier share of energy %.3f"%(r2[3]**2/sum(v*v for v in r2)))
print("MSE %.5f = 2E/n %.5f"%(sum(v*v for v in r)/n, 2*E(a0,b0)/n))
print("balance: one gap of 3 vs three gaps of 1 -> pulls 3 vs 3 (k*gap); energies 4.5 vs 1.5 (k*gap^2/2)")
