The modules

        
Six of them, about four hours, in order.

        
Watch one, make the thing it just showed you. Open any to see inside.

        

          M1 · Thinking in frames 40 min
          

            
What one frame has to do before anything is allowed to move. Why beginner films move too
              much, and the rule that fixes it. After this you can say what is wrong with a frame.

          

        

        

          M2 · Script to shots 45 min
          

            
The sixty-second structure — hook, turn, payoff — and where people stop watching
              if you get the order wrong. After this you can hand back a shot list that renders.

          

        

        

          M3 · Type in motion 50 min
          

            
How type enters, holds and leaves, and why timing beats easing every time. You build your own set of
              moves and learn which ones to refuse. After this your films stop looking like animated slides.

          

        

        

          M4 · Voice and timing 40 min
          

            
Generating the voice, the pass that stops it sounding like a machine, and cutting picture to
              sound. After this the film has a spine and everything hangs off it.

          

        

        

          M5 · Your look 30 min
          

            
Type, colour, grain and 3D as one system you decide once and reuse. How to build a look that
              is yours, and the harder half — knowing what to leave out.

          

        

        

          M6 · Render and ship 35 min
          

            
The gates, export settings that survive compression, cover, caption, subtitles. After this
              you ship weekly without asking anyone.

Questions

        
The things people ask before they pay.

        

          I have never made a video. Too advanced?  
          

            
No. Week one assumes a clean machine and nothing installed. If you can follow a recipe, you
              can do week one.

          

        

        

          Do I need an expensive computer?  
          

            
Any laptop from roughly the last five years with 8 GB of memory runs everything except the
              3D plate at full speed, and that part is optional and renders fine overnight. If your machine
              cannot cope, that is what the refund is for.

          

        

        

          Do I have to be able to draw?  
          

            
No, and you will not learn to. Everything on screen is type, shapes and the rig, all
              described in text. That is the whole method.

          

        

        

          What if I get stuck between reviews?  
          

            
Write to me. Anything that stops you rendering is answered as fast as I can, not held for the
              next review. A blocked week is a wasted week.

          

        

        

          What do I own at the end?  
          

            
The files, for good, including the two demo films as editable source. Use them for your own
              work and for client work. You cannot resell or redistribute the package itself.

          

        

        

          Will my films end up looking like yours?  
          

            
No, and that is deliberate. None of my design ships with this — not my type, not my
              colour, not my rig. The two demo films are built in looks I do not use myself, so there is
              nothing here to copy. What you are buying is the method and a week spent deciding what your
              own films should look like, which is module five and the second review.

          

        

        

          What does it cost me after the $350?  
          

            
Two of the five tools are subscriptions in your own name, not resold through me. Ask before
              you pay and I will send you the exact plans I use and what they cost that week, so you can
              add it up before you decide rather than after.

          

        

        

          Why is there no call?  
          

            
Because you can keep a written review. On a call I would say the same three things and you
              would forget two. In writing you hold it next to your film while you fix it.
