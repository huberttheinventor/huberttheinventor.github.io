Zero bars. Still found.

Four satellites broadcast one thing each: who they are and exactly what time it is. Your phone never replies — it just times the gap between when a shout was sent and when it landed.

The whole mental model, one picture. Everything after it is annotation.

Take it with you: the map as an image

Nº001

The satellite has no idea you exist

Each satellite in the fleet does exactly one job: it broadcasts its own identity and the exact time, on a fixed schedule, into open space. It is not addressed to you. It is not waiting for a reply. It has no way of knowing whether a single receiver on the ground is listening, or a hundred million.

That is the whole trick of "zero bars, still found." A broadcast does not need a connection to the thing receiving it — only a receiver capable of listening. Your phone qualifies. Nothing else about the network has to.

Nº002

Your phone is the finest eavesdropper ever built

Consumer GPS receivers are passive. Your phone never transmits a request to a satellite and never confirms receipt — it only listens, which is also why GPS keeps working with no signal bars and no data plan. There is nothing to authenticate, register, or pay for on the way in.

What it does with what it hears is the actual computation: each satellite's timestamp, compared against the moment its shout arrived, gives one distance. One distance narrows you to a sphere. A second narrows that to a circle. A third pins a point on the circle, and a fourth exists almost entirely to correct your phone's own cheap, sloppy internal clock.

Nº003

Wifi only ever makes it faster, never possible

A GPS fix works with genuinely nothing else — no cellular, no internet, no prior contact with the network at all. When a phone does reach for the internet during a fix, it is asking for the almanac: which satellites are overhead right now, so it is not left scanning empty sky for the first one it can hear. That shortcut is called assisted GPS, and it only ever shortens the wait. It never substitutes for the broadcast itself.

Which is the honest version of "GPS doesn't need a network." Bars are a two-way conversation between your phone and a tower, each side confirming the other heard it. A GPS fix is a one-way sermon — the satellites shout on a schedule whether or not anyone is listening, and your phone is merely in the congregation.
