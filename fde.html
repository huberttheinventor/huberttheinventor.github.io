Sent to live inside your world until it works.

A Forward Deployed Engineer, FDE for short, is the engineer a software company sends to live inside a customer's operation until the product actually works there. Not a sales engineer running a demo. Not a consultant who hands over a slide deck and leaves. An employee who moves onto the customer's floor and stays until the thing runs.

Five systems, no shared language. Everything after it is annotation.

The problem it solves.

Say an aircraft maker buys the platform. Parts data sits in one system. Maintenance logs sit in three more, each in its own format. There's an ERP from 2009 nobody dares touch, a custom API nobody remembers writing, and a spreadsheet called FINAL_v7_REAL that quietly runs the whole company. Nobody can hand over a login and walk away. Someone has to untangle all of it and make it useful.

A year at Airbus.

One account of the work comes from a former Palantir engineer. Nabeel Qureshi spent a year embedded at Airbus in Toulouse, on the factory floor four days a week, according to his essay "Reflections on Palantir" (15 October 2024). He sat with the people doing the work and learned how the place actually ran, which was never quite what the manual said. That's the pattern behind the role: an FDE moves in for weeks or months, connects the systems, writes the code, ships the dashboards, and sometimes builds a whole feature for one customer alone.

Dev versus Delta.

Palantir has its own names for the two engineering tracks. A Dev is one capability serving many customers: an engineer building a piece of the platform itself. A Delta is one customer with many capabilities: an engineer embedded at a single account, doing whatever that account needs. Palantir's own blog post, "Dev versus Delta: Demystifying engineering roles at Palantir" (8 April 2019), is direct about the mix-up people make: a Delta is not a consultant. A Delta builds, and leaves working code in production, not a slide deck.

Why it exploded now.

The role itself is not new, but interest in it is. Indeed job postings for forward deployed engineer were up 543 percent in April 2025 and up 5,230 percent in April 2026, both measured against January 2025, according to Indeed data reported by Business Insider on 18 May 2026. The obvious driver is AI. Nobody wants another chatbot bolted onto their product. Companies want an agent wired into their own customer records, their database, their documents, and their billing, done safely. Somebody has to go in and wire it.

One day on site.

The job's range shows up inside a single day: a Python bug in the morning, a session with the customer's operations team after lunch, and part of the product redesigned by dinner.

The rule of thumb.

The easiest way to keep the three roles straight: an engineer builds the thing. A consultant explains the thing. An FDE moves in and makes the thing work for your exact problem.
