Five machines carry your message. None of them can read it.

Your message is locked on your phone before it leaves. Everything between you and the person you are texting is a courier carrying a box it has no key for. The only thing that ever reads it is the phone in their hand.

The whole mental model, one picture. Everything after it is annotation.

Take it with you: the map as an image

Nº001

It is locked before it leaves you

Encryption happens on your phone, not somewhere in the middle. By the time the message reaches the first server it is already a sealed box, and the keys that open it exist on two devices: yours and the recipient's. Nothing between them is given a copy.

That is the whole reason the rest of the map looks the way it does. Every service after this point is handling something it structurally cannot open, which is a different situation from a company promising not to look.

Nº002

Five services, five different jobs, one sealed box

The chat server holds the open connection your phone never lets go of. The store-and-forward queue keeps the message while the other phone is off in a drawer. Media takes a separate road entirely: a photo is encrypted and uploaded to its own store, and only a pointer and a key travel the chat channel.

The last two never carry the message at all. The push gateway asks Apple or Google to wake the other phone and is not told why. The presence service knows a thumb is moving, which is how "typing…" appears, and nothing else.

Nº003

The envelope is real, and it says a lot

End-to-end encryption protects the contents. It does not hide that a message happened. Who talked to whom, at what time, how often, from which device: that is metadata, it is retained, and it is what actually gets handed over when someone asks.

So the honest version is narrower than "nobody can read your messages". Nobody in the middle can read the words. The pattern of who you talk to and when is a different question, and the answer to that one is not encrypted.
