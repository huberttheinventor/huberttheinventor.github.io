MACOS / THE DEV-OS PICK CARD

The dev-OS pick card: why many developers pick a Mac, and what to weigh against it

No OS wins for everyone. This card lists the five reasons the film gives for the Mac, the honest other side of each, and a rule for deciding for yourself. Facts were checked against vendor documentation on 13 November 2026. Where a point is Apple’s own claim or a widely held opinion rather than a measurement, the card says so.

1. Unix base

macOS has a Unix base: Apple’s own Terminal guide calls the shell “a user interface to the UNIX operating system”. Linux runs about 65% of the websites whose operating system is known (W3Techs survey, 27 November 2026; a survey of websites, not of all servers), so the commands you type on a Mac are close to the ones you use on a server. The card says “Unix base”, Apple’s wording, and does not claim a current UNIX certification, because the Open Group’s brand register did not list a current Apple product when checked.

The other side: WSL is a feature of Windows that runs a Linux environment. WSL 2 runs a real Linux kernel inside a lightweight utility virtual machine, so a Windows developer also gets a Linux terminal. (Apple Terminal guide; Open Group brand register; Microsoft Learn, WSL)

2. Apple Silicon

Apple Silicon puts the CPU, the GPU and the Neural Engine on one chip (a system on a chip). Apple’s claim is speed per watt and long battery life. At the 2020 launch of the M1, Apple said “the world’s best CPU performance per watt” and battery life “up to 2x longer than previous-generation Macs”, using its own benchmarks. These are Apple’s claims, not independent measurements, and this card does not repeat them as fact. (Apple Newsroom, November 2020)

3. The Xcode gate

Xcode, Apple’s developer tool, is listed on the App Store as “Only for Mac”. If you build apps for iPhone, you need a Mac to do it, either on your desk or in the cloud. Apple runs its own Xcode Cloud builds on Apple-run Macs; third-party cloud-Mac services were not checked for this card. (App Store, Xcode listing)

4. One maker

Apple builds the hardware and the software together. Many developers say that makes a polished daily drive with less to go wrong. That is a widely shared opinion, not a measurement: no figures are claimed for build quality, trackpad, display or compatibility.

5. The other side

  • Costs more, and the configuration you pick is the configuration you keep. This is a trade-off, stated without figures, and was not checked against current store pages.
  • Docker on a Mac runs inside a Linux virtual machine, one layer removed from the system you type in. (Docker documentation)
  • Windows suits .NET and desktop apps. Linux suits servers and full control. This is the card’s short summary of where each platform is strongest, a matter of fit rather than a ranking; the only figure behind it is the W3Techs website survey above.

The rule

Your tools. Your workflow. What you’re building. Those three decide the right OS.

The small print: speed per watt and battery are Apple’s claims, not measured. Cost and the locked config are trade-offs, not figures.
