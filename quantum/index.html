A visual guide to measurement

Nothing lands in the middle.

A spin measurement can give two readings. Change how the system is prepared and you change their chances—not the levels themselves.

01 / the record

One dot. One reading.

Each dot is a separate preparation and measurement along one fixed direction. The graphic is a record of results, not a picture of particles travelling through space.

02 / the preparation

The odds can move.

Move the control to prepare a new batch. The two result levels stay fixed. The dots are deterministic illustrations of the stated chance, not a claim that a short sample must exactly match it.

upper result: +ℏ/2 · lower result: −ℏ/2

Upper 50% · lower 50%

03 / the special state

One preparation can make an answer certain.

For this ideal spin-½ measurement along one fixed axis, prepare the upper-result state and every independent trial gives the upper reading: +ℏ/2. That certainty belongs to the state and the chosen measurement together.

That certain reading has a name: an eigenvalue. The special preparation is the matching eigenstate.

not a universal claim

Other observables can have more than two outcomes, or a continuous range. This guide is about one deliberately scoped example.

04 / the mathematical bridge

The rule becomes a number.

The equation is a calculation, not a particle path or physical update. It says that the chosen measurement rule returns this state vector multiplied by one number; it does not say the physical system remains unchanged after measurement.

A v = λ v
A = Sz: chosen measurement rule
v: this special prepared state vector
λ: its certain reading, +ℏ/2 for the upper state

05 / keep the boundary

A useful picture has edges.

  • Dots are independent measurement records.
  • Preparation changes probabilities; it does not rewrite earlier dots.
  • The two-row sample alone does not prove superposition.
  • The equation describes a mathematical relationship, not physical collapse.
