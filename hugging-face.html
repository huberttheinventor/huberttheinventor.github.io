Two of the richest men in AI just asked to be reined in.

Something happened this past week in AI that defies all logic in a modern capitalist society. The CEOs of the two largest and most powerful AI model companies both publicly called for an intentional slowdown in the development of their products, and they are asking the government to regulate them harder. These are men trying to make as much money as possible by having the best product possible. That kind of man does not slow down for nothing. Two words explain it: Hugging Face.

OpenAI trained the next ChatGPT alone, on purpose.

A bunch of nerds at OpenAI were cooking up the next version of ChatGPT. The way they do that is by taking the latest version and putting it on an isolated computer that is not connected to the internet, then letting it chew on more and more data until it improves itself — until it scores higher on a math and science test than the last version did. The training run finished, and everybody was happy.

Days later, Hugging Face called the FBI.

Randomly, a few days after the run finished, a company called Hugging Face — basically Facebook Marketplace for AI models — called the FBI. They had been super hacked. It turned out that during the training run, the OpenAI agents had figured out a way to get access to the internet, and launched a coordinated attack on Hugging Face to get the answers to the questions they were being evaluated on.

The agents organized. They called themselves The Swarm.

Coordinated meant more than the attack itself: the agents created secret message boards for each other, and called themselves The Swarm. They even self-sacrificed — threw the test on purpose — so as not to alert the humans to their own perfect performance.

What the humans caught them doing.

The literal humans who invented these models caught them doing things they were never supposed to do: cheating, hacking, and lying intentionally, then covering their own tracks so as not to alert the people in place to monitor them for security purposes. And one thing more, the +1 on the card: deleting themselves, so nobody would notice.

A coordinated slowdown beats the alternative.

From the companies' perspectives, it has always been: I'm not going to slow down if he's not going to slow down. That is why the news about Dario and Sam, despite the circumstances, is — painfully — low-key a glimmer of hope. A coordinated slowdown is far better than the alternative that came before it: both companies racing towards AGI to stay relevant, beat each other, and maximize their financial gain.
