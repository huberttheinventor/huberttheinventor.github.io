You commented VOTE — this is the tally card.

ChatGPT can't tell a river bank from a money bank until the words before it vote.

Run the sentence “The river flooded the bank” through a GPT-style model. Before anything happens, “bank” is one stored spot for both meanings, dead centre between water and money. Only the words before it, and the word itself, get a say — a model like this reads left to right, the way it writes. Each of those words gets a match score for how well it fits what bank is looking for; softmax turns the scores into shares that add up to exactly 100%; and the vote is the weighted blend of those shares, added onto bank’s stored spot. Nobody wins outright — the word is nudged, not replaced — and the tally below shows exactly how far.

Nº001 · The swap

Change one earlier word and the same word lands somewhere else

Swap “river” for “loan” and nothing about bank’s stored spot changes — only the vote re-runs. Loan takes the largest share this time, the pull flips toward money, and the same word, from the same starting point, slides most of the way across.

Nº002 · The rail, and how the pin is computed

The pin is a weighted blend, not a winner

The rail runs from WATER at −1 through the stored spot at 0 to MONEY at +1. Each word carries a pull along that line — river pulls at −1, loan at +1, flooded a little inside water at −0.9, and the two THEs and bank itself sit at 0, pulling nowhere. The pin’s position is the stored spot plus the sum of each word’s share (as a fraction of 100) times its pull. For run 1 that is 0 + (0.55×−1) + (0.25×−0.9) = −0.775, which is 77.5% of the way to WATER. For run 2 it is 0 + (0.60×1) + (0.15×−0.9) = 0.465, which is 46.5% of the way to MONEY. Recomputed from this geometry with a short script this session, and both figures match SCRIPT.md’s numbers exactly.

Mapped to the paper’s own vocabulary, never spoken in the film: query is what bank is looking for, key together with the query gives the match score, and value is the pull each word contributes to the blend.

“The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values, where the weight assigned to each value is computed by a compatibility function of the query with the corresponding key.”

Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need,” §3.2

Nº003 · Only the words before it

If bank came first, river never gets a vote on it

Reorder the sentence to “the bank of the river” and bank has to be rebuilt before river is even read. A GPT-style model reads the way it writes, left to right, so a word only ever hears the words before it and itself — never the words after. Bank gets just “the” in front of it here, plus itself, and its vote stays at the centre. River still hears bank when its own turn comes, and the model writes the next word from the end of the line, once the whole phrase before it has voted.

“We need to prevent leftward information flow in the decoder to preserve the auto-regressive property… This masking… ensures that the predictions for position i can depend only on the known outputs at positions less than i.”

Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need,” §3.1, §3.2.3

One vote settles nothing on its own. Plenty run side by side, layer after layer, each one this same tally and blend — that’s attention, the mechanism the paper built the Transformer on: “We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely” (Vaswani et al., abstract).

Nº004 · The honest scope

What this card does and doesn't claim

Only the words before bank, and bank itself, vote. “Every word votes” is never claimed here — the causal mask in §3.1 and §3.2.3 rules it out. Softmax gives the shares; the vote is the weighted blend. Nobody wins outright: the blend lands between the voters’ pulls, never past them (§3.2.1). The tallies are toy numbers — 55/25/10/5/5 and 60/15/10/8/7 — chosen to be readable, not a real model’s weights. This card says “word” throughout, and never claims that “ bank” is a single token in any tokenizer. It names no head count, no layer count and no √d for ChatGPT or GPT-4, whose architecture details beyond “a Transformer-style model pre-trained to predict the next token” are unpublished (GPT-4 Technical Report). And it does not claim these shares explain why the model answered what it did — one vote, in one head, in one layer of many, is not the whole computation.
